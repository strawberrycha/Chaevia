Career Compass

A web-based decision support system that helps Senior High School students explore college courses, built for school guidance offices.

My role: project leader. I planned the project, designed the scoring algorithm, and built the interface, with co-researcher Juno Alligah B. Romano.

  • Next.js
  • Tailwind CSS
  • GSAP
  • jsPDF
  • Vercel
  • 85 courses ranked
  • 4.77 / 5 overall rating (ISO/IEC 25010)
  • 30 teachers and guidance designates evaluated it
  • 2 languages plus offline mode
Career Compass live

The problem

Guidance is manual and paper-based, and counselors also carry a full teaching load. Students struggle to choose a college course. Of 30 respondents interviewed, 25 named limited money as the most common student problem.

The solution

8 interest scenarios, 15 skill statements, 4 academic steps; then top five matches with reasons, explore and compare all 85 courses, scholarship information, and a one-page PDF report. Supports both the previous and the Strengthened SHS curriculum.

How the scoring works

each course has fixed links to interests, skills, and academic performance; the system adds up matches and ranks all 85 courses; every course has the same number of links; the same answers always give the same ranking; rule-based weighted scoring, NOT AI; three guidance designates validated the questions and rules.

Design decisions

no login and no database; answers stay on the student's device (fits the Data Privacy Act of 2012); Offline Mode and English/Filipino switch came from the interviews; the journey-map interface makes it feel like exploring.

Process and testing

Rapid Application Development (requirements planning, user design, construction, cutover); all four component test scenarios passed; link check confirmed 15 links for each of the 85 courses.

Results

Reliability 4.83, functional suitability 4.77, interaction capability 4.73; overall weighted mean 4.77 (Strongly Agree).

Limitations

a supporting reference, not a final decision; the evaluation involved teachers and guidance designates, not students; covers only its 85 listed courses; next steps are testing with students and adding courses.
